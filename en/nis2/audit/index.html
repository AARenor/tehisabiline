NIS2 audit in Estonia: scope, evidence and report

NIS2 does not create one mandatory audit certificate. It creates duties that must be evidenced. This guide shows what an audit should cover, which artifacts matter and how to avoid a document dump that proves nothing.

By Arle Aron Reidma, Tehisabiline OÜ · Last materially updated 24 October 2026

Short answer

NIS2 does not prescribe one audit certificate or one audit format. The obligated entity must be able to evidence that risk management, the ten duties, supply-chain security, incident handling and management accountability operate. An audit is a structured check of that evidence; a checklist is only a starting point.

Audit scope

  • Risk: asset and risk register, treatment plan, owners.
  • Ten duties: access, cryptography, continuity, backup, vulnerability handling, MFA and more.
  • Incidents: detection, 24/72-hour reporting and final report.
  • Supply chain: due diligence, contract clauses and subcontractors.
  • Governance: board approval, training and accountability.
  • Evidence: dates, owners, test results and corrective actions.

Evidence that matters

NIS2 audit evidence
DutyEvidence
Risk analysisRegister with likelihood, impact, treatment and owner
Incident handlingLog, notification history and tabletop record
ContinuityBackup restore test and RTO/RPO
Supply chainSupplier inventory, due diligence and contract clauses
AccessRole review, MFA coverage and privileged access
GovernanceBoard minutes, risk appetite and training records

A policy with no operating evidence is weak. Prefer a small number of dated, owned artifacts over a large unverified folder.

How an audit runs

  1. Scope: entities, services, systems and critical suppliers.
  2. Evidence request: a short list mapped to the ten duties.
  3. Interviews and sampling: test whether the control operates.
  4. Findings: impact, evidence, fix and owner.
  5. Report: priorities and a retest option.

Start with the registration and incident process if those are not yet documented.

Common gaps

  • Risk register exists, but no treatment dates or owners.
  • MFA is deployed, but coverage is not measured.
  • Backups run, but restore is not tested.
  • Suppliers are listed, but not assessed.
  • Board approval is missing or undated.
  • Incident reporting exists only on paper.

Review the broader cybersecurity audit scope or request a scoping call.

Frequently asked questions

Does NIS2 require a mandatory audit certificate?

No. NIS2 does not create one mandatory audit certificate. Supervisors and customers can ask you to evidence that the ten duties operate; an internal or independent audit can produce that evidence.

What does a NIS2 audit cover?

Risk analysis, incident handling, continuity and backup, supply-chain security, access control, training, cryptography, vulnerability handling, MFA and governance.

Is a checklist enough?

No. A checklist shows intent. An audit checks evidence that the control operates, with an owner, date and result.

How long does it take?

It depends on scope and evidence quality. A focused review can take days; a multi-entity group with weak evidence takes longer. Agree the scope in writing first.
