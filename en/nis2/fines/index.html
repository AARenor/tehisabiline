NIS2 fines in Estonia: caps, supervision and liability

The caps are legal maximums, not expected fines. This guide explains the amounts, how a supervisor weighs a breach, what management accountability means and how to reduce exposure before a review.

By Arle Aron Reidma, Tehisabiline OÜ · Last materially updated 27 October 2026

Short answer

Essential entities: up to €10 million or 2% of the previous financial year's worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher. Important entities: up to €7 million or 1.4% of the same turnover, whichever is higher. These maximums apply to breaches of the security-measure duties (KüTS § 7) and the incident-reporting duties (KüTS § 8), under KüTS § 182 and § 183. A fine is not automatic: the supervisor weighs severity, duration, prior breaches, impact, intent or negligence, mitigation and cooperation (KüTS § 14(7)). For scope and duties, see NIS2 in Estonia.

What the supervisor assesses

KüTS § 14(7) lists the factors. They are assessment criteria, not a prediction of a fine:

  • Severity and duration of the breach.
  • Prior breaches and warnings.
  • Impact on services, customers and other persons.
  • Intent or negligence.
  • Mitigation, cooperation, notification and remediation speed.

Documenting decisions and fixes is the evidence that the entity took the duty seriously.

Management accountability

NIS2 Article 20 requires management bodies to approve and oversee cyber-risk measures. National law determines personal or board-level consequences. The practical lesson: board minutes, risk appetite, budget decisions and remediation deadlines should be traceable, not reconstructed after an incident.

Read the full NIS2 guide for the ten duties and incident reporting.

How to reduce exposure

  1. Confirm scope and register with RIA on time; see NIS2 registration.
  2. Keep a risk register with owners and treatment dates.
  3. Test backup restore, MFA and incident contact routes against the 24- and 72-hour clocks.
  4. Collect supplier and technical evidence.
  5. Record board decisions and remediation progress.

Frequently asked questions

What is the maximum NIS2 fine?

For essential entities, up to €10 million or 2% of the previous financial year's worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher. For important entities, up to €7 million or 1.4% of the same turnover, whichever is higher. These are maximums, not expected fines.

Is a NIS2 fine automatic?

No. The supervisor weighs severity, duration, prior breaches, impact, intent or negligence, mitigation and cooperation (KüTS § 14(7)). A fine is one possible measure, alongside warnings and binding instructions.

Can management be held liable?

NIS2 Article 20 requires management bodies to approve and oversee cyber-risk measures. National law determines personal or board-level consequences; do not assume a fine is the only risk.

Does a small company avoid NIS2 fines?

No automatic immunity. If a small company is in scope, its duties apply. Size matters for scope: the general rule is at least 50 employees and turnover or balance sheet above €10 million, but some categories apply regardless of size.
