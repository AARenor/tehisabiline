NIS2 incident reporting in Estonia: 24, 72 hours and 1 month

A significant incident starts a clock, not a writing project. This guide explains the three reporting stages, what to include, who to notify and how to avoid losing time on perfect information.

By Arle Aron Reidma, Tehisabiline OÜ · Last materially updated 24 October 2026

Short answer

24 hours: early warning. 72 hours: incident notification. One month: final report. The clock starts when you become aware of a significant incident. Do not wait for a complete root-cause analysis before sending the early warning; refine it later.

Timeline

NIS2 incident reporting stages
StageDeadlinePurpose
Early warning24 hoursAlert the authority and CSIRT; initial impact and contact.
Notification72 hoursAssess severity, affected services and initial response.
Final report1 monthRoot cause, impact, measures and lessons.

Keep a dated log of detection, decisions and submissions. The timeline is evidence too.

What to include

  • Entity: legal name, contact and affected services.
  • Incident: what happened, detection time and initial cause.
  • Impact: operational, financial, customer and cross-border effects.
  • Response: containment, workarounds and current status.
  • Contact: a person who can answer follow-up questions.

Send what you know, mark uncertainty explicitly, and correct it in the next report.

Process

  1. Detect and declare: name an incident lead and start the log.
  2. Triage: decide whether it is significant and which services are affected.
  3. Warn: submit the 24-hour early warning through the official channel.
  4. Investigate: contain, preserve evidence and prepare the 72-hour notification.
  5. Close: submit the final report and record corrective actions.

Run a tabletop before you need this process. See training and tabletop.

Common errors

  • Waiting for a complete root cause before the 24-hour warning.
  • Notifying only the IT team and not the legal/communications owners.
  • No evidence preservation: logs overwritten during containment.
  • Understating cross-border or supplier impact.
  • No record of the submission or follow-up.

Frequently asked questions

What are the NIS2 incident reporting deadlines?

An early warning within 24 hours of becoming aware of a significant incident, a notification within 72 hours, and a final report within one month.

Who do I notify in Estonia?

Notify the competent authority and CSIRT through the channel specified by RIA. Use the current official route and keep the submission confirmation.

What is a significant incident?

An incident that causes severe operational disruption, financial loss or harm to others. The assessment is fact-specific; do not wait for perfect information before sending the early warning.

What should the early warning contain?

What happened, when it was detected, affected services, initial impact, cross-border effects and a contact. It can be refined in later reports.
