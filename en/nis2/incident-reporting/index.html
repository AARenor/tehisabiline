NIS2 incident reporting in Estonia: 24, 72 hours and 1 month

A significant incident starts the 24-hour and 72-hour clocks when you become aware of it. The one-month final report starts when you file the 72-hour notification. This guide explains the three stages, what to include and who to notify. For the wider picture, see NIS2 in Estonia.

Short answer

For a significant-impact incident, send the early warning without undue delay and no later than 24 hours after becoming aware, and the incident notification without undue delay and no later than 72 hours after becoming aware (KüTS § 8(1) and (41)). The final report is due within one month of filing the 72-hour notification (§ 8(7)). If the incident is still unresolved then, the report you file counts as a progress report, and a new final report is due within one month after the incident is resolved. Do not wait for a complete root-cause analysis before sending the early warning.

Is the incident significant?

The short summary is not the full test. Under KüTS § 8(2), an incident has significant impact if at least one of these conditions is met:

  1. its impact is at least serious (raske) on the severity scale of the system risk analysis under § 7(1)(1);
  2. the service cannot continue after the maximum allowed outage time set in its service-level agreement or service-continuity requirements;
  3. the incident disrupts the continuity of another service provider's service;
  4. resolving it requires emergency measures described in the risk analysis or another service-recovery document;
  5. significant damage has occurred or may occur for the provider, another provider or service users;
  6. it meets the significant-incident condition in a European Commission implementing act under Directive (EU) 2022/2555, Article 23(11).

Under § 8(3), an incident that disrupts a service in at least one other EU Member State is always treated as significant. Read the full text on Riigi Teataja (KüTS § 8).

"One month" means the same calendar date and time in the next month. If that date does not exist in the next month (for example 31 January), the deadline is the last day of the next month at the same time. This is a date-calculation aid, not a legal determination.

Example (not a legal determination)

Inputs: awareness on 3 November 2026 at 10:00; 72-hour notification filed on 5 November 2026 at 14:00.

Formula: 3 Nov 10:00 + 24 h = 4 Nov 10:00 (early warning); 3 Nov 10:00 + 72 h = 6 Nov 10:00 (notification deadline); 5 Nov 14:00 + one calendar month = 5 Dec 2026 14:00 (final report).

Legal basis: KüTS § 8(1), (41) and (7).

What to include

  • Entity: legal name, contact and affected services.
  • Incident: what happened, when it was detected and the initial cause, if known.
  • Impact: operational, financial, customer and cross-border effects.
  • Response: containment, workarounds and current status.
  • Contact: a person who can answer follow-up questions.

Send what you know, mark uncertainty and correct it in the next report. The data and procedure are set by regulation under KüTS § 8(8); check RIA's current channel before filing. The NIS2 audit page describes the evidence workbook you can use to keep supporting records.

Process

  1. Detect and declare: name an incident lead and start a dated log.
  2. Triage: decide whether the incident meets the conditions in § 8(2) or (3).
  3. Warn: submit the early warning through RIA's channel, CERT-EE Raport, no later than 24 hours after becoming aware.
  4. Notify: submit the incident notification no later than 72 hours after becoming aware. Record the time you file it: the one-month final-report period starts then.
  5. Close: submit the final report within one month of the notification. If the incident is still open, the report counts as a progress report and a new final report follows after resolution.

Not yet registered with RIA as a service provider? See NIS2 registration.

Personal-data breaches (GDPR)

GDPR is a separate duty. A controller notifies the Estonian Data Protection Inspectorate (AKI) within 72 hours only if the personal-data breach is likely to result in a risk to people's rights and freedoms (GDPR Article 33(1)). A processor notifies its controller without undue delay (Article 33(2)). NIS2 and GDPR triggers are assessed separately. Source: GDPR, EUR-Lex.

Common errors

Late or missing reports can lead to fines; see NIS2 fines. The points below are practical recommendations, not legal requirements.

  • Waiting for a complete root cause before the early warning.
  • Counting the final-report month from the wrong event; it runs from the filing of the 72-hour notification.
  • Notifying only the IT team and not the legal and communications owners.
  • Overwriting logs during containment; preserve evidence first.
  • Understating cross-border or supplier impact.
  • No record of the submission or follow-up.

Frequently asked questions

What are the NIS2 incident reporting deadlines?

Early warning without undue delay and no later than 24 hours after becoming aware of a significant-impact incident. Incident notification without undue delay and no later than 72 hours after becoming aware. Final report within one month of filing the 72-hour notification.

Who do I notify in Estonia?

RIA, through the CERT-EE Raport channel. Keep the submission confirmation.

What is a significant incident?

An incident that meets at least one condition in KüTS § 8(2), or that disrupts a service in another EU Member State (§ 8(3)). Read the full test in Riigi Teataja. Do not wait for perfect information before sending the early warning.

What should the early warning contain?

What happened, when you became aware, affected services, initial impact, cross-border effects and a contact. You can refine it in later reports.

Can RIA ask for a progress report?

Yes. RIA can request a progress report before the final report is due (§ 8(4³)).

Do I notify AKI about every personal-data breach?

No. A controller notifies AKI within 72 hours only if the breach is likely to result in a risk to people's rights and freedoms. A processor notifies its controller without undue delay.
