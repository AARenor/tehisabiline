NIS2 in Estonia: short answer

NIS2 is the EU directive on cybersecurity risk management. Estonia implemented it through the Cybersecurity Act (Küberturvalisuse seadus, KüTS), in force from 1 January 2026. The main cybersecurity authority is RIA.

If your activity is in a category of the Cybersecurity Act (KüTS) § 3 and the size test for that category is met, or the category applies regardless of size, you must notify RIA, apply the ten minimum risk-management measures, manage supply-chain risk and report significant incidents. Entities already in scope on 1 January 2026 had three months to notify RIA; that window closed in spring 2026, so anyone who has not notified RIA should do it now. Existing providers have three years from 1 January 2026 (until about 1 January 2029) to bring their operations into line with the Act. Vital-service providers follow the deadline set under the crisis and national defence act (KORKS) § 83(4)(3).

The practical work is not a policy binder. It is a risk register, an incident process, tested backups, access control, supplier evidence and a management decision trail.

Does NIS2 apply to your organisation?

Apply a two-step test: sector (is your main activity in a category listed in KüTS § 3?) and size (does that category's size test apply, and do you meet it?). Some categories apply regardless of size. A sector label alone is not enough, and size alone is not enough.

Short NIS2 scope check

Answer four questions. The check runs only in your browser; nothing is uploaded.

1. Is your organisation a public-sector body or a public-law legal person, or do you carry out public tasks (for example as a vital-service provider)?
2. Is your main activity in a category listed in KüTS § 3 (for example energy, transport, banking, health, water, digital infrastructure, managed ICT services, postal, waste, food, manufacturing or research)?
3. Does your organisation have at least 50 employees AND annual turnover or balance sheet above €10 million?

This is the general size test for most categories; some categories use higher thresholds. Partner and linked-enterprise figures are not automatically added (KüTS § 3(7)).

4. Do you provide a service covered regardless of size?

For example DNS, top-level domain registry, qualified trust service, critical communications network, public electronic communications or a vital service. Check the exact definition in KüTS § 3.

NIS2 applicability: criteria, assessment and dates

The applicability question has a fixed shape, and it is worth answering it in writing rather than by intuition, because the answer decides whether registration, the ten measures and the reporting chain apply to you at all.

  1. Criterion 1 — the category. Your main activity must appear in KüTS § 3. Being a software vendor, a consultancy or a marketing agency is not on the list; being a managed service provider, a data centre, a hospital or a water utility is.
  2. Criterion 2 — the size test. Most categories need 50 or more employees and annual turnover or a balance sheet above €10 million. Some categories apply regardless of size: DNS and top-level domain registries, qualified trust services, critical communications networks and operators of vital services.
  3. Criterion 3 — no exclusion hides the duty. A group subsidiary that provides services independently of its affiliates is assessed on its own systems, not on the parent's numbers.

Dates. KüTS has applied since 1 January 2026. Entities already in scope registered with RIA within three months of that date; a newly obligated entity has three months from the moment it meets the criteria, and 14 days to report a change in its data. Bringing operations into line with the measures runs three years from entry into force, to roughly 1 January 2029. The reporting clock is much shorter: 24 hours for the early warning and 72 hours for the incident notification after you become aware of a significant incident.

Assessment output. A defensible applicability assessment is one page: the category with the KüTS § 3 reference, the employee and turnover figures with their financial year, the conclusion (essential, important, or not in scope), the date, and the named person who signed it. The Estonian-language version of this test, with the full § 3 category table, is on the NIS2 Eestis page; the ten measures are listed in the NIS2 compliance checklist.

RIA registration

Registration is a notification to RIA, not a certificate of compliance. Log in to the business view of eesti.ee, open Küberturvalisus → Teenuseosutaja teavitus and submit the requested data.

  1. Confirm scope: the KüTS § 3 category, its size test and any group relationships that affect scope.
  2. Prepare contacts: an address and contact route that work during an incident. A 24/7 contact is a recommendation, not a registration requirement.
  3. Submit accurate data: name and register code, operating address, current e-mail, telephone and IP-address ranges, and the sector and subsector.
  4. Report changes: notify RIA no later than two weeks after a change.
  5. Save proof: confirmation, submission time, submitter and a copy of the data.

A new service provider must notify RIA within three months of meeting the criteria (KüTS § 41(1)). Entities already in scope on 1 January 2026 had three months from that date; that window closed in spring 2026. If you have not notified RIA, do it now and record why it was late. Registration opens a supervisory dialogue; it does not replace the risk assessment or the evidence pack. For the step-by-step guide, see NIS2 registration in Estonia.

The ten NIS2 duties

Article 21 sets minimum areas for risk-management measures. They are deliberately broad: the organisation must decide how each area applies and keep evidence that the measure works.

  1. Policies on risk analysis and information-system security.
  2. Incident handling.
  3. Business continuity, such as backup management and disaster recovery, and crisis management.
  4. Supply-chain security, including security aspects of relationships with direct suppliers and service providers.
  5. Security in network and information-system acquisition, development and maintenance, including vulnerability handling and disclosure.
  6. Policies and procedures to assess the effectiveness of the risk-management measures.
  7. Basic cyber-hygiene practices and cybersecurity training.
  8. Policies and procedures on cryptography and, where appropriate, encryption.
  9. Human-resources security, access-control policies and asset management.
  10. Multi-factor or continuous authentication, secured voice, video and text communications, and secured emergency communication systems, where appropriate.

Use the NIS2 checklist (Estonian) to map each area to an owner, evidence and a test date.

Management duties (Article 20)

Management duties are separate from the ten measures. The management body must approve the risk-management measures, oversee their implementation and follow cybersecurity training (NIS2 Article 20). Under KüTS § 61, a board member approves and oversees the measures and is responsible for them. NIS2 sets no training interval, so an annual refresher is an internal target, not a legal requirement.

Incident reporting: 24 hours, 72 hours and one month

The reporting duty applies to a cyber incident with significant impact (KüTS § 8(2)). The 24-hour and 72-hour clocks start when you become aware of the incident. The one-month final report starts from the filing of the 72-hour notification. In Estonia, reports go to RIA through its reporting channel (Raport, CERT-EE).

NIS2 reporting timeline (clocks are statutory; the content column is a practical checklist)
WhenReportPractical content (suggested)
Within 24 hours of becoming awareEarly notice (esmane teade)What happened, when it was detected, whether it is ongoing or cross-border, initial severity and a contact.
Within 72 hours of becoming awareIncident notification (intsidenditeade)Affected systems and users, initial assessment, indicators and measures already taken.
One month after the 72-hour notification is filedFinal report (lõppraport)Root cause, impact, mitigation, recovery and measures to prevent recurrence. If the incident is still unresolved, the report counts as a progress report, and a new final report is due within one month after resolution.

Record the time, sender, content and acknowledgement of every report. Reporting does not replace containment, recovery or legal advice. Voluntary reports of near misses are possible under KüTS § 81. GDPR is a separate track: the controller notifies the Data Protection Inspectorate (AKI) within 72 hours unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to people's rights and freedoms, and a processor notifies its controller without undue delay. For the step-by-step version, see NIS2 incident reporting in Estonia.

What audit evidence looks like

NIS2 does not create a single mandatory audit certificate. Supervisors and customers can ask you to show that the measures operate. A credible evidence pack usually contains:

  • Risk register: assets, threats, likelihood, impact, treatment and owner.
  • Management decision: board minutes, approved risk appetite, budget and remediation deadlines.
  • Technical evidence: configuration, access reviews, MFA, backup restore tests, logging and vulnerability handling.
  • Supplier evidence: due diligence, contractual security requirements and review notes.
  • Incident evidence: exercise reports, reporting records and lessons learned.
  • Training evidence: attendance, content and phishing-exercise results.

The step-by-step evidence list is in the NIS2 audit guide. A penetration test can test a system; it cannot replace governance, documentation or a supplier process. See the audit scope guide (Estonian).

Fines and supervision

Fines are maximum amounts (ülempiir), not predicted penalties. They apply to breaches of the security-measure and incident-reporting duties in KüTS § 7 and § 8. For essential entities the maximum is €10 million or 2% of the previous financial year's worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher. For important entities it is €7 million or 1.4% of the previous financial year's worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher. Supervisors can also issue warnings, binding instructions, remediation orders and temporary restrictions.

NIS2 maximum fines (KüTS § 182 and § 183)
Entity typeMaximum fine
Essential entity€10 million or 2% of the previous financial year's worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher
Important entity€7 million or 1.4% of the previous financial year's worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher

The bigger operational risk is often downtime, data loss and lost customer trust. A fine is not automatic; the supervisor weighs severity, duration, impact, repeat breaches, intent or negligence, mitigation and cooperation (KüTS § 14(7)). It is also not the only cost of a weak incident process.

30-day readiness plan

A suggested sequence, not a legal timetable.

  1. Days 1–3: confirm scope (KüTS § 3 category and size test), group relationships and registration status; name an accountable owner.
  2. Days 4–7: build or refresh the asset and risk register; identify the top five scenarios.
  3. Days 8–14: test backup restore, MFA, privileged access and the incident contact route.
  4. Days 15–21: collect supplier and technical evidence; fix the highest-risk gaps.
  5. Days 22–30: test the incident process with your own team, record board decisions and schedule the next test.

Frequently asked questions

What are the NIS2 applicability criteria?

Two criteria plus one exception. The main activity must be in a category listed in KüTS § 3, and most categories then require 50 or more employees together with annual turnover or a balance sheet above €10 million. Some activities apply regardless of size: DNS and top-level domain registries, qualified trust services, critical communications networks and operators of vital services.

Do NIS2 requirements apply to our suppliers?

Indirectly, and this is where most gaps appear. If you are in scope, Article 21(2)(d) makes supply-chain security your duty: you must assess and contract for the security of the providers you depend on. Your customers will pass the same expectation down, which is why providers outside NIS2 scope still receive security questionnaires and contract clauses.

Is NIS2 already in force in Estonia?

Yes. Estonia's NIS2 transposition in the Cybersecurity Act (KüTS) took effect on 1 January 2026. RIA is the main cybersecurity authority.

When was the NIS2 registration deadline?

Entities already in scope on 1 January 2026 had three months from the amendment's entry into force on that date to notify RIA. That window closed in spring 2026. If you have not notified RIA, do it now through eesti.ee.

Does NIS2 require an ISO 27001 certificate?

No. ISO/IEC 27001 is voluntary. It can support an information-security management system, but the certificate does not by itself prove NIS2 compliance.

What are the incident reporting deadlines?

An early notice at most 24 hours after you become aware of a significant incident, an incident notification at most 72 hours after you become aware, and a final report within one month after the 72-hour notification is filed. In Estonia, reports go to RIA through the prescribed channel.

What fines can RIA impose?

These are maximum amounts. For essential entities: up to €10 million or 2% of the previous financial year's worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher. For important entities: up to €7 million or 1.4% of the previous financial year's worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher.

Does NIS2 require an audit?

NIS2 does not prescribe one audit certificate. Supervisors can ask for evidence that the ten risk-management measures work. An internal or independent audit is one way to test that.
