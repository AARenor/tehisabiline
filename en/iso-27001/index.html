ISO 27001 in Estonia: certification and evidence

ISO/IEC 27001 is a voluntary international standard for an information-security management system. This guide explains the certification path, the evidence auditors ask for and where ISO 27001 fits with NIS2 and E-ITS.

ISO 27001 in Estonia: short answer

ISO/IEC 27001 is a voluntary international standard for an information-security management system (ISMS); the current edition is ISO/IEC 27001:2022 including Amendment 1:2024. Certification is issued by an accredited certification body after a two-stage audit, with surveillance under that body's scheme. It is not a legal licence and it does not automatically prove NIS2, E-ITS or DORA compliance.

The standard is useful when a customer, procurement or market expects a recognised management-system certificate. It also forces a repeatable cycle of risk assessment, controls, measurement and improvement.

Certification path

  1. Scope: legal entities, locations, systems and services in the ISMS.
  2. Gap analysis: compare current practice with ISO 27001 clauses and Annex A controls.
  3. Risk assessment: identify assets, threats, vulnerabilities and treatment.
  4. Implementation: policies, access control, suppliers, incidents, continuity and evidence.
  5. Internal audit and management review: test the ISMS and record decisions.
  6. Stage 1 and Stage 2: documentation review, then implementation audit.
  7. Surveillance and recertification: the interval and validity period come from the certification body's accredited scheme; periodic surveillance is the common practice, not a rule set by the standard itself.

The transition deadline for ISO/IEC 27001:2013 certificates was 31 October 2025 and has passed (IAF transition table).

Evidence auditors ask for

  • ISMS scope, policy and risk methodology.
  • Risk register, treatment plan and statement of applicability.
  • Asset, access and supplier inventories.
  • Incident, continuity and backup test records.
  • Internal audit, management review and corrective actions.
  • Training, awareness and competence records.
  • Metrics showing whether controls work.

A document set without operating evidence is the most common reason a certification audit stalls.

ISO 27001, NIS2 and E-ITS

NIS2 is a legal duty; E-ITS is the Estonian framework; ISO 27001 is an international management-system standard. They overlap but are not interchangeable. Map each NIS2 duty to an ISO 27001 control and a proof, and do not assume a certificate covers incident reporting or supervisory cooperation.

Common mistakes

  • Writing policies before understanding the actual risks.
  • Treating the statement of applicability as a copy-paste list.
  • No owner for suppliers, incidents or access reviews.
  • Internal audit that only checks documents, not operation.
  • Certification scope that excludes the systems customers actually rely on.

Frequently asked questions

Is ISO 27001 mandatory in Estonia?

No. ISO/IEC 27001 is a voluntary international standard. A customer, procurement or sector rule may require it, but it is not a universal legal obligation.

Does ISO 27001 prove NIS2 compliance?

No. An ISO 27001 certificate can support an information-security management system, but NIS2 also requires governance, incident reporting, supply-chain management and supervisory evidence.

How long does certification take?

It depends on scope and maturity. A focused certification project often takes several months, including gap analysis, implementation, internal audit, management review and the certification audit.

Can Tehisabiline issue an ISO 27001 certificate?

No. Certification is issued by an accredited certification body. We provide technical testing and the evidence it produces, such as a scoped security audit report, but we do not perform ISMS consulting and we make no certification decision.
