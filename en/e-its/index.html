E-ITS in Estonia: audit, measures and NIS2

E-ITS is Estonia's information-security framework. This guide explains who must use it, what changed in 2026, how the audit works and how to connect E-ITS evidence to NIS2 without pretending they are the same thing.

E-ITS in Estonia: short answer

E-ITS is the Estonian information-security standard maintained under RIA's framework. It provides a catalogue of security measures, a method for selecting them and an audit model. It is not a product certificate and it is not automatically the same as NIS2 or ISO/IEC 27001.

For an organisation under KüTS, E-ITS can be the practical way to structure the ten NIS2 risk-management areas. For an organisation outside the mandate, it remains a useful reference, but the legal obligation must be checked separately.

Who must use E-ITS?

Organisations performing public tasks in Estonia must apply E-ITS (RIA overview). KüTS service providers have mandatory basic security measures, and where the baseline is insufficient they must additionally use E-ITS measures; that duty follows the applicable regulation and the provider's protection need, not the standard alone.

For other organisations, E-ITS use is voluntary: it can structure risk management and evidence but imposes no legal duty by itself. Start with the legal question: does a statute, supervision decision, public procurement or contract require E-ITS? If not, decide whether E-ITS or ISO 27001 better fits your evidence and customer expectations.

What changed in 2026

The 2026 version was established by regulation no. 30 of 25 August 2026 and took effect on 1 September 2026 (E-ITS 2026 requirements). The catalogue is organised in nine module groups (GRC, ORP, OPS, DER, DEV, INF, NET, SYS, APP), and RIA's change log states the former BSI base-threat catalogue was replaced (change log).

Audit timing follows Government Regulation no. 121 and the entity's service-provider category; the current audit rules also allow an updated audit conclusion to be submitted to the supervisory authority between audits. Do not state a fixed cycle without checking that regulation. The practical effect is fewer overlapping audit steps, but not less evidence: the organisation still needs a scope, risk assessment, selected measures, implementation evidence and a remediation plan.

Measures and scope

E-ITS is modular: the organisation selects a baseline, adds modules for its technology and risk profile and documents justified exclusions. Typical evidence includes:

  • Scope and asset inventory.
  • Risk assessment and selected baseline.
  • Access control, logging, backup and update evidence.
  • Supplier and incident processes.
  • Internal review, test results and remediation.
  • Management approval and ownership.

Do not treat the catalogue as a checklist to fill once. The selected measures must match the actual system and be tested.

How the E-ITS audit works

The audit is performed by an auditing entity whose lead auditor holds a recognised qualification (CISA, IRCA or PECB ISO/IEC 27001 lead auditor) and audit experience, and the team must be independent of the auditee's design and implementation work (audit rules, points 4.2–4.6). It is not a penetration test and does not replace technical testing. Typical preparation:

  1. Define the scope and the applicable baseline.
  2. Map each selected measure to an owner, evidence and test date.
  3. Close obvious gaps and document accepted risks.
  4. Run an internal review against the audit questions.
  5. Prepare the evidence pack and the remediation log.

The auditee submits the audit conclusion to the supervisory authority within 14 days of receiving the conclusion and final report (6.17), together with the final report where the conclusion cites a high-level risk (6.18). A high-level risk must be remedied within six months, with notification duties if that fails (7.3–7.5).

We are not an E-ITS auditing entity. If you need technical control evidence alongside the E-ITS material, we can run a security audit or penetration test; that is not a formal E-ITS audit and does not replace one.

E-ITS and NIS2

NIS2 is the legal duty; E-ITS is one way to organise the security measures. A mapping table should connect each NIS2 area to the selected E-ITS measures and a proof. Do not claim that an E-ITS audit automatically satisfies NIS2: governance, incident reporting, supply-chain evidence and supervisory cooperation still need their own proof.

See NIS2 in Estonia for the ten duties, registration and reporting timeline.

What an auditor will ask for

  • Approved scope and risk assessment.
  • Selected measures and justified exclusions.
  • Configuration, logs, access reviews and backup restore tests.
  • Supplier and incident records.
  • Internal audit or review results.
  • Remediation plan with owners and deadlines.

Frequently asked questions

Is E-ITS mandatory in Estonia?

E-ITS is mandatory for organisations performing public tasks in Estonia. KüTS service providers have mandatory basic security measures and must additionally use E-ITS measures where the baseline is insufficient. For other organisations it is voluntary; the obligation depends on the entity and the applicable legal rule.

What changed in E-ITS 2026?

The 2026 version took effect on 1 September 2026 and organises the catalogue in nine module groups. The audit rules set the 14-day submission of the audit conclusion, the six-month remedy for a high-level risk and the option of an updated conclusion between audits; the audit interval itself follows Government Regulation no. 121 and the entity's category.

Does E-ITS prove NIS2 compliance?

No. E-ITS can provide evidence for many security measures, but NIS2 also requires governance, incident reporting, supply-chain management and supervisory evidence. Map each NIS2 duty to an E-ITS measure and a proof.

Is E-ITS the same as ISO 27001?

No. E-ITS is the Estonian information-security framework; ISO/IEC 27001 is an international management-system standard. They can complement each other, but neither certificate automatically proves the other.

How often is an E-ITS audit required?

The audit interval follows Government Regulation no. 121 and the entity's service-provider category; the 2026 audit rules also allow an updated audit conclusion to be submitted to the supervisory authority between audits. Confirm the applicable requirement with RIA and that regulation.
