Cybersecurity audit in Estonia: scope, evidence and checklist

A scoped review of access, configuration, suppliers and the evidence behind your security claims. We separate an audit from a penetration test and give you a remediation plan your team can own.

Audit or penetration test?

An audit asks whether the organisation has the right controls, evidence and ownership across systems and processes. A penetration test asks whether a specific system can be exploited under controlled conditions. An audit can reveal a missing access review; a test can reveal that an endpoint lets you bypass it.

Some compliance programmes need both. Neither replaces a risk assessment or a management decision.

What we review

  • Identity and access: privileged accounts, MFA, joiners/leavers and access reviews.
  • Configuration: endpoints, logging, backups and update paths.
  • Suppliers: critical providers, contracts, concentration and exit evidence.
  • Incident readiness: contacts, classification, reporting and exercise records.
  • Evidence: policies that match practice, ownership and test dates.

The scope is written before work starts. We avoid broad access to personal data and use read-only evidence where possible. Cloud configuration can be included in an agreed scope; not a separate cloud-specialist offer.

What a cybersecurity audit covers

"Cyber security audit" is used for at least three different engagements, and the phrase itself says nothing about depth. The useful distinction is what gets tested:

Three engagements that share the name
EngagementQuestion answeredTypical evidence
Compliance evidence reviewDo we hold the artefacts NIS2, E-ITS or ISO 27001 expect, and are they current?Register and sample exports, policies with approval dates, access review minutes
Technical control reviewDo the controls operate: MFA coverage, logging, patching, restore success?Configuration extracts, backup restore log, patch report, ticket samples
Penetration testCan a specific system be exploited under controlled conditions?Findings with reproducible steps and impact

Scope that names no systems, no evidence owners and no sample size is a promise, not an audit. A defensible scope lists the systems, the control set, the sampling method, the excluded areas with a reason, and who on your side owns each evidence request.

The coverage areas we work through, in the order they usually produce findings: identity and privileged access; endpoint and server configuration; logging and detection; backup and restore; supplier and cloud dependency; incident readiness; and the governance layer that approves and reviews all of it.

Preparation checklist

Audit cost is decided before the first meeting: a client who can produce evidence in a week pays for a week. Six items cover most of the difference:

  1. Asset and system list with owners, not a CMDB export nobody trusts.
  2. Access review evidence for privileged accounts, with decisions and dates.
  3. One successful restore with a log — the single most common missing artefact.
  4. Patch and vulnerability report showing the window you actually meet, not the one in the policy.
  5. Supplier list with criticality, contracts and the last due-diligence note.
  6. One closed incident end to end: detection, decision, communication, final report.

If the legal driver is NIS2, prepare against the ten measures using the NIS2 compliance checklist. For a financial entity under DORA, the register of information and ICT risk evidence come first — see the register of information guide.

Auditor independence and credentials

Neither "cyber security audit" nor "NIS2 audit" is a protected title in Estonia, so credentials matter less than three structural facts: whether the reviewer is independent of the team that built the controls, whether the report names the evidence it checked, and whether the same provider also sells the remediation. All three can be legitimate — but the conflict has to be visible and the report has to be usable by someone who was not in the room.

For an essential entity under NIS2 this is not academic: RIA may run its own security audit up to once every three years under KüTS § 16(11)(21), and a board has to be able to explain what was reviewed and what changed. That is why the deliverable matters more than the badge.

Process

  1. Scoping: systems, legal driver, evidence owners and constraints.
  2. Evidence collection: interviews, configuration extracts and samples.
  3. Validation: test a sample of controls and follow the evidence trail.
  4. Findings: severity, business impact, root cause and owner.
  5. Remediation plan: priority, effort and a proposed retest.

What the report contains

  • Scope, limitations and evidence sources.
  • Management summary with the top risks.
  • Findings with evidence, impact and recommended fix.
  • Prioritised remediation plan and owners.
  • Mapping to NIS2, E-ITS or ISO 27001 where relevant.
  • Retest results where agreed.

We do not issue a generic "compliant" certificate. The report states what was reviewed and what remains uncertain.

NIS2, E-ITS and ISO 27001

An audit can support the evidence pack for NIS2 in Estonia, E-ITS or ISO 27001, but the legal obligation and certification decision remain separate. We map each finding to the relevant control and evidence owner.

Frequently asked questions

What is a cyber security audit?

A structured review of whether the organisation has the right controls, the evidence that they operate and a named owner for each. It samples systems and processes rather than exploiting them, and it ends in findings with evidence and a remediation plan, not in a certificate.

Is there an accredited cyber security auditor?

There is no single mandatory licence for a cybersecurity audit in Estonia. Recognised qualifications exist for specific schemes, such as ISO 27001 lead auditor for certification work, but the useful test is independence from the controls being reviewed, a written scope, and a report that names the evidence it checked.

What is the difference between an audit and a penetration test?

An audit reviews governance, access, configuration, suppliers and evidence across systems. A penetration test attempts controlled exploitation of a specific scope. They answer different questions and can complement each other.

Do you need access to production?

Not always. We agree read-only access, screenshots or evidence exports. The scope should minimise access to personal and customer data.

Can an audit prove NIS2 compliance?

An audit can provide evidence, but NIS2 compliance also depends on governance decisions, incident reporting, supplier management and supervisory requirements. We map findings to the relevant duties rather than issue a generic certificate.

What do we receive?

A scope document, findings with evidence and severity, a management summary and a prioritised remediation plan. Retesting can be added.

How long does an audit take?

It depends on the number of systems, locations and evidence owners. A focused review can take days; a multi-site audit takes longer.

Frameworks and sources

An audit verdict is only checkable if the criteria are named. These are the reference points we map findings to.

  • ISO/IEC 27001 — management-system criteria when a client uses ISO as the baseline.
  • NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 — the govern/identify/protect/detect/respond/recover structure used in the management summary.
  • Riigi Infosüsteemi Amet (RIA) — E-ITS baseline and Estonian supervisory practice.
  • Directive (EU) 2022/2555 (NIS2) — article 21 measures and article 23 incident duties.
