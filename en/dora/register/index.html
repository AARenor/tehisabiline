DORA · Article 28(3)

DORA register of information: template, ITS fields and deadline

The register is the evidence that you know which ICT third-party providers support which functions, under which contracts and with what exit risk. This guide covers the fields, a working CSV and the mistakes that break supervisory reporting.

Short answer

Article 28(3) of DORA requires a register of information on all contractual arrangements for ICT services provided by third-party service providers. The register must show the provider, the contract, the function supported, the data and systems involved, the exit strategy and the risk. The ITS 2024/2956 template is the supervisory format; a working CSV is only an internal collection tool.

What the register must show

  • Provider: legal name, identifier, country, group and chain of subcontractors.
  • Contract: reference, start and end, renewal, termination and governing law.
  • Function: the business or ICT function supported and whether it is critical or important.
  • Data: personal data, confidential data and data location.
  • Systems: the systems and processes that depend on the service.
  • Risk: concentration, substitutability, exit plan and supervisory relevance.

Working template and official format

Use a controlled working file to collect evidence, then map it to the official ITS template before submission. A working CSV is useful because contracts, owners and systems change; the official file is the reporting artifact.

Download the register of information template (CSV)

The template carries the Article 28(3) fields a supervisor asks for — provider, subcontractor, supported function, criticality, data location, contract dates, exit plan, risk level, owner and evidence — with two sample rows to delete. For the Estonian submission context and the difference between the working CSV and the official ITS template, see DORA in Estonia and the Estonian register guide.

The fourteen ITS templates

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2956 (OJ L, 2.12.2024) sets the supervisory format. It is not one spreadsheet but fourteen related templates, linked by shared keys:

ICT service types carry coded identifiers S01 to S19 in Annex III, and Annex II maps each type of financial entity to its activities. Both are reported as identifiers, not free text, which is why a working file that only lists provider names cannot be mapped to the official format without rework.

The four keys that link the templates

The templates are open tables — a fixed set of columns and an indefinite number of rows — joined by four identifiers. Get these wrong and every downstream template breaks:

  1. Contractual arrangement reference number, unique per arrangement between the signing entity and the direct provider.
  2. Identifiers of the financial entities and providers. Providers established in the EU are identified by LEI or EUID; providers outside the EU by LEI only. Natural persons acting as providers may use an alternative code.
  3. Function identifier, chosen by the entity from its own function taxonomy, at entity and group level.
  4. Type of ICT service, from the S01–S19 list in Annex III.

Group consistency is a reporting requirement, not a preference: the same contract reference, function identifier, financial-entity LEI and provider identifier must hold across entity, sub-consolidated and consolidated registers. A group may keep one register covering all three levels, which is usually cheaper than reconciling three.

Excel, CSV or the official format

Three files get confused, and only one of them is a regulatory deliverable:

  • A working sheet (Excel or CSV) collects evidence while contracts, owners and systems change. It should mirror the template codes so mapping later is mechanical.
  • The ITS template is the submission structure: fourteen linked tables with coded values.
  • A single flat spreadsheet is neither. It cannot represent B_02.02 (services and functions per arrangement) or B_05.02 (subcontractor chains) without losing relationships.

Practical order: build the working sheet by template code, one tab per B_ template, then export. The free CSV template covers the Article 28(3) fields in one flat table — good for collecting the data, explicitly not the submission format.

Deadline: annual, and what the next collection covers

Article 28(3) requires the register to be maintained and updated continuously, with an annual report of summary data to the competent authority and the full register on request. There is no single EU-wide submission date in the regulation: the national competent authority sets the collection window. For Estonian financial entities the annual collection runs through Finantsinspektsioon, and the next cycle reports the position at 31 December 2026.

Two consequences follow. First, the register is a running record, not a December project: an arrangement signed in March has to be in the file before the year-end snapshot. Second, the register is not the annual report — the summary still has to be prepared, including the number of new arrangements in the period.

How to fill it

  1. Start from contracts, not a spreadsheet. Collect signed contracts, statements of work and change orders.
  2. Assign one owner per row. The business owner confirms the function; legal confirms the contract; security confirms data and access.
  3. Map subcontractors. Do not hide a critical subcontractor inside a provider row.
  4. Record the exit plan. A register without substitutability and exit evidence is incomplete.
  5. Review before reporting. Reconcile rows with the contract register and the incident/asset inventories.

Common errors

  • Treating the CSV as the official submission.
  • One row per provider instead of one row per contractual arrangement.
  • Missing subcontractors or data locations.
  • Marking every service as non-critical to avoid scrutiny.
  • No named owner, review date or evidence link.
  • Copying last year's register without checking changes.

Submission in Estonia

In Estonia the register is submitted to Finantsinspektsioon through its data-collection portal, following FI’s current instructions. This is separate from serious ICT incident reporting, whose channel changes on 1 November 2026: email is accepted until 31 October 2026, the portal is the route from 1 November 2026.

Article 28(3) requires an annual report with summary data, including the number of new arrangements; the full register is provided on the supervisor’s request. Finantsinspektsioon separately collects a year-end register, with the 2027 collection using the position at 31 December 2026. Confirm the current format and deadline before sending.

For the reporting data model, see EBA’s DORA register of information resources.

Frequently asked questions

Where can I download a DORA register of information template?

Tehisabiline publishes a free CSV template with the Article 28(3) fields: provider, registry code or LEI, subcontractor, supported function, critical or important flag, data location, contract start and end, exit plan, risk level, owner and evidence. It is a working file for collecting the data, not the official ITS 2024/2956 submission format. Download the CSV template.

Is the DORA register of information the same as the ITS template?

The register is the substantive record required by Article 28(3). The ITS 2024/2956 template is the supervisory format used to submit that record. A working CSV helps you collect the data, but it does not replace the official template.

Who must maintain the register?

Financial entities in scope of DORA must maintain a register of information on all contractual arrangements for ICT services provided by third-party service providers.

How many templates does the register of information have?

Fourteen, coded B_01.01 to B_07.01 in Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2956. They cover the entity maintaining the register, the entities in scope, branches, contractual arrangements (general, specific and intra-group), the signing entities and providers, the entities using the services, the providers, the supply chain, the function identifiers and the assessments of each ICT service.

Which identifier must an ICT provider have?

A provider established in the EU is identified by LEI or EUID; a provider established outside the EU by LEI only. Natural persons acting as ICT providers may use an alternative identification code. The same identifier has to hold across every template and at group level, so it is worth fixing before the first row is entered.

How often should it be updated?

Keep it current as contracts, providers and functions change. Article 28(3) requires an annual report with summary data, including the number of new arrangements, and the full register on the supervisor's request. Finantsinspektsioon collects a year-end register separately; the 2027 collection uses the position at 31 December 2026.

Does a completed CSV prove DORA compliance?

No. The register is one evidence artifact. DORA also requires governance, ICT risk management, incident processes, resilience testing and contractual safeguards.
