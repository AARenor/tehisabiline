Where a conversational AI consultancy actually helps

Most failed assistants are not model failures. They fail because the use case is too broad, the source data is not trusted, permissions are unclear, nobody defined a good answer, or the team cannot measure quality after launch. We work on those decisions before adding more prompts.

Typical engagements: a customer-support assistant over approved documentation, an internal knowledge assistant with access control, a sales or onboarding assistant, or an evaluation layer for a system already in production.

Process

  1. Scope: users, decisions, data, permissions and what a wrong answer costs.
  2. Baseline: current process, source quality and measurable success criteria.
  3. Build: retrieval, prompts, tools, guardrails and fallback in short iterations.
  4. Evaluate: a test set from real questions and known failure modes.
  5. Harden: access control, logging, monitoring, cost limits and ownership.
  6. Hand over: documentation, runbook and a backlog the team can own.

Evaluation before scale

We define what “good” means before tuning the model: answer correctness, citation or source fidelity, retrieval recall, refusal when evidence is missing, latency and cost per resolved task. The test set is versioned so changes can be compared rather than argued about.

Read the AI security testing guide for the threat side, or the AI Act guide for the regulatory timeline.

Security, privacy and the AI Act

  • Data-flow and permission model before connecting internal sources.
  • Prompt-injection and tool-use review for assistants that act, not only answer.
  • Logging with retention limits; no silent training on customer data.
  • Intended-purpose and role mapping for AI Act screening.
  • Clear boundary: we provide technical evidence, not legal advice or a compliance certificate.

Frequently asked questions

Do you build chatbots or production assistants?

Both, but the scope differs. A chatbot can be a focused interface; a production assistant needs retrieval, permissions, evaluation, logging, fallback and an owner after launch.

Can you work with our existing models and cloud?

Yes. We are model-agnostic and can work with hosted or self-hosted models, your cloud account and your data boundaries. The architecture follows the use case and the evidence you need.

How do you evaluate a conversational assistant?

We define a test set from real questions and failure modes, then measure answer correctness, retrieval quality, refusal behaviour, latency and cost. Evaluation continues after launch.

Does an AI assistant need an AI Act review?

Not every assistant is high-risk, but the use case, role and data flows determine the obligations. We map the intended purpose and flag where legal review is needed; we do not provide legal advice.
