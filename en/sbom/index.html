SBOM for CRA: components, vulnerabilities and evidence

A software bill of materials is the inventory behind CRA vulnerability handling. This guide covers the legal minimum, practical fields, SPDX and CycloneDX, release linkage and the evidence a customer or market-surveillance authority may request.

SBOM for CRA: short answer

The CRA requires manufacturers to identify and document the components of a product with digital elements and to handle vulnerabilities throughout the support period. An SBOM is the practical inventory that makes this possible.

An SBOM is not a one-off PDF. It should be tied to a product release and, where available, include transitive dependencies. The CRA's minimum is a machine-readable SBOM covering at least top-level dependencies (Annex I, Part II, point 1); it belongs in the technical documentation and is provided to a market-surveillance authority on request. Deeper dependency coverage is good practice, not the legal floor. It must connect to a vulnerability process that can find, triage and fix affected components.

SPDX, CycloneDX or a spreadsheet?

SPDX and CycloneDX are the common machine-readable formats. Choose one your build pipeline can generate and your customers can consume. A CSV is useful for a first inventory, but it is harder to validate and link to releases.

Whatever the format, keep a stable mapping between an internal component ID and the package identifier. That mapping is what lets you answer a new CVE without rebuilding the whole inventory.

A workable SBOM process

  1. Generate: produce the SBOM in the build pipeline, not after release.
  2. Enrich: add supplier, identifiers, licenses and dependency relationships.
  3. Store: keep each version with the product release and build metadata.
  4. Match: compare components with vulnerability feeds and advisories.
  5. Triage: assign an owner, exploitability and remediation deadline.
  6. Update: regenerate when a component changes; keep the old version for traceability.
  7. Share: provide the agreed extract to customers or authorities under contract.

Vulnerability matching in practice

A CVE feed alone is not enough. You need a component-to-advisory mapping, a triage decision and evidence of the fix. Track:

  • Affected product and version range.
  • Exploitability and exposure in your configuration.
  • Severity and business impact.
  • Remediation, workaround and release version.
  • Customer communication and disclosure timing.

For actively exploited vulnerabilities, CRA reporting deadlines apply. Keep the SBOM and the incident record linked.

What to show a customer or regulator

  • SBOM for the exact release, with a timestamp.
  • Component identifiers and supplier contacts.
  • Vulnerability policy, triage records and remediation SLAs.
  • Evidence of the fix and the new SBOM version.
  • Support-period statement and update mechanism.
  • Disclosure and incident-reporting records.

Frequently asked questions

Is an SBOM mandatory under the CRA?

The CRA requires a machine-readable list of components in a commonly used format covering at least top-level dependencies (Annex I, Part II, point 1), kept in the technical documentation and provided to a market-surveillance authority on request. It does not prescribe one file format and does not require publication.

Which SBOM format should I use?

SPDX and CycloneDX are the most common machine-readable formats. Choose what your tooling and customers can consume, and keep the mapping to internal component IDs.

Should the SBOM be public?

Not necessarily. Regulators, customers and integrators may need an extract under contract. Publish only what you intend to disclose; the internal SBOM can remain confidential.

Does an SBOM include transitive dependencies?

The CRA's minimum covers top-level dependencies; including transitive dependencies is good practice and gives a truer picture of the attack surface. Direct components alone hide the dependency chain and the vulnerabilities that reach your product through third-party libraries.

How often should the SBOM be updated?

At every release and whenever a component changes materially (a practical cadence, not a statutory one). It should stay tied to the product version and support period.

Does an SBOM prove CRA compliance?

No. It is one evidence layer. Compliance also needs risk assessment, secure design, vulnerability handling, technical documentation, conformity assessment and support.
